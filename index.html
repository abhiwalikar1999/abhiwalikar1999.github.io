About me

Intelligent systems have always made me curious. How are they built? How does a model "think" and arrive at a decision? I wondered about this for a long time, until one day I asked myself: why not build one? That question still drives my machine learning work, where I care just as much about understanding a model's decisions as about its accuracy.

With LLMs, this question became even bigger for me, because an answer can look right and still be wrong. That's why I focus on making LLM systems reliable, with structured outputs, retrieval, self-checks and testing against real examples.

How I work

  1. Measure first, then improve

    Before changing my LLM pipeline I built a hand-labelled test harness. Every later change was scored against it.

  2. Report results honestly

    Including the unflattering ones: an 8B model that ran 4.6× faster but scored 0.00 F1, and a black-box model whose 86.1% test score was 75.9% under cross-validation.

  3. Make it usable by others

    Tests, Docker, CI and a live deployment, so the work runs outside my laptop and anyone can check it.
